        About

        llmledger reads the full git history of LiteLLM's model price file every day, and the Arena leaderboard for quality. It estimates what a prompt costs, which model is the best value, and how likely each price is to change.

        What the estimates leave out

        Prompt tokens are counted at about 4 characters per token; each model counts its own way, often 10 to 30% differently. Thinking models also bill hidden reasoning as answer tokens. Caching, batch discounts and long prompt prices are left out. When LiteLLM has no per-image or per-second price, an image counts as about 1,000 tokens, a PDF page about 1,500, a second of audio about 32 and a second of video about 300. "How many times" is how often you send the prompt; in a chat, each message also re-sends the conversation so far.

        Compare side by side: higher effort makes a thinking model write more hidden answer tokens, and no open source measures how many at each level yet, so the table starts every model at the answer length you picked; type the tokens you see in your own use to compare. On a Claude or ChatGPT plan you pay no per-token price; the table assumes a plan is used up in proportion to each model's API price, because plans do not publish per-model limits.

        What is Arena?

        Arena (arena.ai, started at UC Berkeley in 2023) shows people two answers to their own prompt from two anonymous AI models, side by side. They vote for the better one.

        From over a million votes, each model gets a score, like a chess rating: about 1,500 is typical, and higher is better. A 100 point lead means people preferred that model about 64% of the time.

        The range under a score is a 95% interval. When two ranges overlap, the votes cannot tell the two models apart. This page uses Arena's scores with style control, which keeps long or heavily formatted answers from winning on looks alone.
