        Aalen-Johansen estimates of competing risks with 1,000 bootstrap resamples. Each row adds up to 100% because the exits compete: once a model is removed, its price can no longer change. Download as CSV.

        Does the definition matter?
        $sensitivity
        Every change, or leaving out corrections?

        Leaving out unit fixes and undone changes ignores a price made 100 times larger or smaller and a price change undone within 30 days, and treats a model removed in a bulk clean up (50 or more removed on the same day) as still followed until that day. Leaving out likely corrections as well also ignores a price change in a model's first 7 days, one from a price of 0, and one of 10 times or more either way. Neither finds every correction: in a labelled sample of 200 first price changes, about 4 in 10 of those the second one keeps were still corrections, so both overstate how often a host really changes a price.

        $corrections

        Changed in the last 7 days

        Tags mark bulk clean ups (50 or more models removed on the same day), unit fixes (a price made 100 times larger or smaller), reverted changes (undone within 30 days) and likely corrections (a price change in a model's first 7 days, from 0, or of 10 times or more: 9 in 10 such first price changes were corrections in a labelled sample).

        Most repriced

        Changes from one price to another; first prices, unit fixes, changes undone within 30 days and likely corrections are not counted.

        Hosts most likely to change a price within a year

        Only hosts with at least 100 models still followed at one year.

        Which makers' models change first

        The same model and method as Over time, split by the maker the naming rules give each model: for every maker with at least 100 models added since September 2023, the estimated share whose first change within a year was each one, with a 95% bootstrap interval, while at least 20 of its models are still followed. Makers with fewer models are pooled. A model's maker can be wrong where the naming rules are.

        Did LiteLLM's guard change the share of corrections?

        An interrupted time series: each week's price changes and how many of them carry a correction tag (unit fix, reverted, likely correction), 26 weeks before the guard and every week after, in a segmented logistic regression (a trend, a step at the guard, a change of slope) weighted by the week's changes. The interval resamples whole weeks, because one bot sync can change hundreds of rows in a week. The guard checks that the price file and its backup match and that the file fits a schema, not that a price is right, so no change is the expected answer; measuring it is the point.

        About

        llmledger reads the full git history of LiteLLM's model price file every day, and the Arena leaderboard for quality. It estimates what a prompt costs, which model is the best value, and how likely each price is to change.

        How often is the automatic task guess right?
        What the estimates leave out

        Prompt tokens are counted at about 4 characters per token; each model counts its own way, often 10 to 30% differently. Thinking models also bill hidden reasoning as answer tokens. Caching, batch discounts and long prompt prices are left out. When LiteLLM has no per-image or per-second price, an image counts as about 1,000 tokens, a PDF page about 1,500, a second of audio about 32 and a second of video about 300. "How many times" is how often you send the prompt; in a chat, each message also re-sends the conversation so far.

        Compare side by side: higher effort makes a thinking model write more hidden answer tokens, and no open source measures how many at each level yet, so the table starts every model at the answer length you picked; type the tokens you see in your own use to compare. On a Claude or ChatGPT plan you pay no per-token price; the table assumes a plan is used up in proportion to each model's API price, because plans do not publish per-model limits.

        Many price changes in the catalogue fix a wrong entry rather than change what a host charges: in a labelled sample of 200, about 6 in 10 first price changes were such corrections (86% of those in a model's first week). The chances of a price change on this page count them as price changes. Price history also shows the chance leaving out likely corrections, and tags them; the tags miss some corrections, so that chance is still too high.

        What is Arena?

        Arena (arena.ai, started at UC Berkeley in 2023) shows people two answers to their own prompt from two anonymous AI models, side by side. They vote for the better one.

        From over a million votes, each model gets a score, like a chess rating: about 1,500 is typical, and higher is better. A 100 point lead means people preferred that model about 64% of the time.

        The range under a score is a 95% interval. When two ranges overlap, the votes cannot tell the two models apart. This page uses Arena's scores with style control, which keeps long or heavily formatted answers from winning on looks alone.
